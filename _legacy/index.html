Different Thinking

reSrch/wiki · last edited 2026
An AI research lab building products for people who think differently.

Different Thinking is an artificial-intelligence research lab that designs tools around cognitive difference rather than against it. Its work draws on ideas from neurodiversity, cognitive science, and human–computer interaction, and is organised around the belief that the outliers in a system — the bugs — are usually the most interesting signal.

History

The lab grew out of research into machine learning systems that adapt to individual users rather than a statistical average. Early prototypes explored how large language models could be tuned to a single person's patterns of attention and memory.

Its founding thesis — that difference is a feature — is often traced to earlier arguments in the neurodiversity movement and to accounts of unconventional problem-solving by figures such as Alan Turing and Temple Grandin.

Research

Published work spans reinforcement learning from human feedback, personalised recommendation, and interface design for dyslexia and ADHD. A recurring theme is treating error states not as failures to be hidden but as data to be studied.

The lab's shader and rendering experiments — the glitching specimens shown above — are part of a research programme on generative visual systems and WebGL.

Alex

Alex is the lab's AI coaching product: a conversational coach that adapts to how an individual actually thinks. Rather than enforce one workflow, Alex learns a person's rhythms and reflects them back, drawing on techniques from cognitive behavioural therapy and metacognition.

Philosophy

The company slogan, "Your Bugs are Cool," restates a position long held in hacker culture: that anomalies are where understanding begins. The lab frames software bugs and cognitive quirks as the same phenomenon viewed at different scales.

Reception

Commentators have compared the lab's output to glitch art and to the studio tradition of treating research as a public artefact.
