    A diagnosis, or someone's lack of one. Medication and health. Mental-health crises. Money, jobs and relationships going wrong. A missed deadline, inside the product. Anyone who isn't in on it.

    ApproachObjects, team, campaign
    1 / 5
            
    The world around the glitch is calm: chrome, paper, grey cloth and daylight. That restraint is what lets the glitch read as a feature rather than noise.
    Objects are shot like museum pieces. People are shot plainly, in black and white, and never retouched into someone else.
    
      
    Always5 rules
    Quiet grounds
    Grey sweeps, cloth backdrops and Paper. The ground stays neutral so the glitch can be loud.
    Chrome and daylight
    Objects are metal or white, lit softly from one side with a real shadow.
    One subject
    One object, one person or one poster per frame, with room left for the logotype.
    Black and white people
    Team portraits are black and white, square-on, on grey cloth.
    Colour comes from the work
    The only saturated colour in frame is the glitch, a product or a screen.

    CursorMove over it, any direction
    Detail · 4×
    • Every direction counts. Intensity builds toward any edge, top, bottom or either side, and settles in the middle.
    • Direction steers it. Side to side tears the slices left or right, up and down slides the pixel stretch with you, and the echo leans toward the pointer.
    • Ease toward it. Everything closes 8% of the gap each frame, so it swells and settles instead of jumping. No flips at the halfway mark.
    • No hover, no problem. On touch, a ghost cursor drifts through the frame so the reaction is still seen.

    PrinciplesFour rules
      
          
    • Calm at rest. The glitch idles quietly and reacts to the viewer. It never loops at full intensity.
      • 
          
    • Snap in, settle out. Arrivals are fast and uneven. Exits are gentle.
      • 
          
    • Glitch is an event. Use it for moments, never as a constant background behind reading.
      • 
          
    • Mind the eyes. No full-screen flashes. Anything with strobing carries a photosensitivity warning.
